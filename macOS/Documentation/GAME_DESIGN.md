# Game Design Direction

## Core fantasy

The player is a tiny "Poison Patroller" dropped into exaggerated household danger zones. Each screen looks like a lost public-service arcade game from roughly 1988–1994: black backgrounds, radioactive green graphics, chunky type, warning stripes, scanlines, short PSA slogans, and deliberately corny transitions.

## Gameplay loop

Each stage is a fixed 4:3 screen built from platforms and ladders. Hazardous household items are scattered across the platforms. The player navigates to each one and presses Space at close range to cover it with a green Mr. Yuk-style sticker. Toxic-drip enemies patrol platforms and cost a life on contact.

Completing every hazard triggers a PSA interstitial before the next episode.

## Tone

The game should feel sincere and slightly weird rather than cynical. The humor comes from the production style: ominous beeps, giant warnings, CRT flicker, melodramatic chapter names, and cheerful scoring for obviously responsible actions.

## Visual rules

- Logical canvas: 640×480, 4:3
- Dominant colors: black, electric green, warning yellow, off-white
- Typeface: monospaced system font for a period-computer feel
- UI should look like an old educational kiosk or local-TV PSA bumper
- Avoid modern gradients and glossy controls
- Keep sprites simple, readable, and slightly chunky

## Educational beats

Stage cards currently reinforce three simple ideas: medicine is not candy; chemicals should be stored up and out of reach; unknown substances should not be tasted or touched. The final card displays the U.S. Poison Help number, 1-800-222-1222, and directs the player to call 911 for collapse or trouble breathing.
